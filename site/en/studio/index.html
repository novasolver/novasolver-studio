Simulation for everyone

Strength and heat analysis used to belong to companies with expensive CAE licences and specialists to run them. Now anyone can run it by asking an AI. A shape an AI comes up with is checked by an AI against the laws of physics, so the path from idea to physical verification becomes one continuous step, and AI moves from writing text and drawing pictures to checking whether a thing will actually work.

Analysis you can run yourself

With a PC and an AI agent you can compute the strength, heat and flow of a shape you just thought of. At work or at home.

No high-end CAE licence

The software is free and open source. No licence fees, no seat limits.

What checking with CAE gets you

It turns "looks strong enough" into "a design checked by calculation", for a company's products and for things you make yourself.

Optimise the shape

The AI makes a shape, Studio evaluates it, and the AI reshapes it from the result. Each round moves you toward a part that is lighter and uses less material while staying strong.

Say "safe up to X kg" with a number

Stress, deflection and margin to the material's strength under the load you expect, as numbers checked for mesh convergence. One basis for the ratings you publish.

Example: a 3D-printed shelf bracket (PLA), back fixed, 500 N (about 50 kg) on the shelf → 0.15 mm deflection (model with uniform, solid material)

Stand out from look-alikes

Among products that look the same, "a design checked by simulation" is a reason to choose yours. Show it in catalogues, quotes, shop pages and crowdfunding campaigns.

Build fewer prototypes

Do the make-break-fix loop on screen first, and spend material and time only on the prototypes you really need.

Simulation results support a design; they are not a warranty. Before you guarantee a product, account for material scatter and the manufacturing process, and add physical tests where needed.

Ask your AI assistant to solve it

Studio speaks MCP (Model Context Protocol). Ask Claude or another assistant: "Fix the bottom face of this part, push 500 N on the top, and tell me whether it is verified." The assistant sets up and runs the case and reports back. Its edits appear live in the GUI, so you can review them.

There is no general shell or code-execution tool. The AI never writes solver files directly; it fills in checked input fields only.

Getting started (Windows)

  1. Download the zip from GitHub Releases and unpack it (no installation).
  2. Double-click NovaSolverStudio.exe. The app opens in your browser.
  3. Structural and thermal analyses work right away (built-in solvers and CalculiX are included).
  4. For flow, install OpenFOAM® (ESI Windows package, about 200 MB) to %LOCALAPPDATA%\NovaSolverStudio\solvers\openfoam. No admin rights needed.

The preview is not code-signed yet. If Windows shows a warning, choose "More info → Run anyway". The zip's SHA-256 is listed on the Releases page.
