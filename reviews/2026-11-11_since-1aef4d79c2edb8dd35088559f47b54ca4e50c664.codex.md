### High — Structural thermal loads are silently omitted

**What goes wrong:** A structural case with mechanical loading and a uniform thermal load of 300 °C is optimized using only its mechanical loading. An in-memory comparison confirmed identical Genesis loads and stiffness after adding the thermal load, with no ignored-condition warning.

**Why:** `build()` transfers surface forces but never calls `structural.temperatures()` or includes thermal expansion forces. `precheck()` accepts the case. The existing ignored-load warning covers gravity and rotation only.

**Suggested fix:** Reject active structural thermal loads with a translated scope error until Genesis supports their forces and sensitivities.

### High — Contact pairs bypass the contact restriction

**What goes wrong:** The built-in `hertz-cylinder` case passes Genesis precheck despite its frictionless contact pair. Genesis then optimizes a voxel continuum without the separation and sliding behavior specified by that pair. Where the voxels meet, they share displacement degrees of freedom.

**Why:** Precheck examines `case["contacts"]` but overlooks `case["contact_pairs"]`. `_mesh_for_run()` creates `_contact_pairs` records, but Genesis never uses them when constructing its stiffness operator.

**Suggested fix:** Reject separate-surface contact pairs before starting the job unless their behavior is explicitly represented. Even bonded pairs require deliberate treatment of separate meshes and gaps.

*Evidence: precheck executed; downstream behavior confirmed by code-path inspection.*

### High — STL extraction severely thins retained members

**What goes wrong:** A member retained by a lowered binary threshold is analyzed as full voxel material, but its STL can become much thinner. In a probe with a one-cell ligament at density 0.3, the analyzed thickness was 0.25 m; the closed extracted surface was only approximately 0.000998 m wide.

An actual cantilever optimization with 30 iterations produced an STL containing approximately 67.4% of the binary voxel volume used for its reported stiffness.

**Why:** Binarization selects its own threshold, but `surface()` still extracts at 0.5. Retained cells below 0.5 are raised only to 0.501, placing their surface intersections almost at their centers. The threshold warning claims volume and load paths are preserved, which this surface construction does not ensure.

**Suggested fix:** Extract from the final binary part, or remap densities around the selected threshold with controlled interface placement. Check exported thickness, connectivity, and volume against the analyzed part.

### High — STEP holes contribute positive volume

**What goes wrong:** In-memory construction of a rectangular ring produced a reported volume of `4.0800000004e-7 m³`, while the actual OpenCASCADE solid had volume `6.5200000001e-7 m³`. The discrepancy occurred for extrusion along every axis tested.

**Why:** The plane-surface/extrusion construction yields a solid whose kernel mass adds the hole contribution. The final calculation reports outer area minus hole area instead of checking the constructed solid. The comment acknowledging the kernel discrepancy does not correct its geometry.

**Suggested fix:** Construct holes through explicit Boolean subtraction or correct the wire/shell orientation. Require kernel volume to agree with the intended polygon volume before offering the STEP file.

*Evidence: construction executed with disk writing intercepted; serialized STEP round-trip was not run.*

### High — Thermal cleanup fails to detect removed heat input

**What goes wrong:** For a thermal grid with a fixed-temperature end, opposite-end heat input, and no kept boundary cells, optimization at volume fraction 0.1 removed material from every heated node. It reported a maximum temperature rise of approximately 2.73 K, while the input nodes reached approximately 774.87 K in the ersatz-material solution. No `load_not_carried` warning appeared.

**Why:** `clean()` checks loaded-node connectivity only for elastic problems. Thermal problems retain `loads_carried=True`. Final temperature maxima then exclude nodes outside the retained solid, hiding the removed heat-input region.

**Suggested fix:** Check heat-input connectivity as well as mechanical-load connectivity. Reject or explicitly flag disconnected thermal inputs, and avoid presenting retained-solid temperatures as a valid solution for those inputs.

### Medium — CAD import selects older geometry

**What goes wrong:** Send an edited `Body.step` as a new case before that case has a Genesis result. If an older `Body.step` case has a result, Import Genesis can insert the older geometry with `stale: false`. An in-memory probe confirmed this selection.

**Why:** `find_cases()` appends filename matches after case-ID and content-hash matches. Both CAD integrations select the first candidate **with a result**, allowing a filename-only match with different contents to replace the intended case. Additionally, a cold-start send returning `None` leaves the previous stored case ID intact.

**Suggested fix:** Resolve the intended case before looking for its result. Do not fall back to different-content filename matches when a case-ID or hash match exists. Clear obsolete case IDs when a new send cannot return one.

### Medium — Empty designs crash finalization

**What goes wrong:** The thermal probe at volume fractions 0.03 and 0.05 completed optimization but failed during finalization with:

`ValueError: zero-size array to reduction operation maximum which has no identity`

**Why:** Thresholding and cleanup can leave no solid cells. `_finish()` nevertheless computes displacement or temperature maxima over the empty retained-node mask.

**Suggested fix:** Detect an empty final part before computing maxima. Return a translated infeasible-design error that suggests increasing material volume or keeping boundary regions.

### Medium — Genesis staleness is not refreshed after edits

**What goes wrong:** Finish Genesis, change a load or geometry dimension, then return to the run step. The previous shape and metrics remain visible without the stale warning until Genesis is reloaded through another action.

**Why:** Saved edits, external case refreshes, and undo update the case payload and analysis results but do not refresh Genesis. Its `stale` flag remains the value fetched before the edit.

**Suggested fix:** Update Genesis staleness whenever the case revision changes. Include the run revision in the result payload or refresh its summary, and guard in-flight responses against revision changes.
