## 1. High — Mesh verification tracks prescribed current

**What goes wrong:** Import a conductor, prescribe `1 A` on one face, ground another, disable Joule heating, and request mesh convergence. The study tracks approximately `−1 A` on every mesh and can set `verified=true` without assessing voltage or current-density accuracy.

An in-memory reproduction used a 10 × 4 × 1 mm plate with a through-hole of radius 1.8 mm. Meshes containing 164, 436 and 1,494 elements produced a current GCI of approximately **1.1 × 10⁻⁹%**, while power changed from **109.969 → 112.502 → 112.697 µW**.

**Why:** With one prescribed-potential level, no resistance metric is created. `primary_quantity()` therefore selects `current_a`, which is the balancing reaction to the prescribed current. Conservation fixes this quantity regardless of discretization accuracy. `run_study()` then promotes its GCI into verification for imported cases. The prescribed-charge fallback has the same problem when total charge is imposed.

**Suggested fix:** Track a response quantity: potential difference, power or effective resistance for prescribed-current cases; energy or potential for prescribed-charge cases. Keep conservation checks separate from mesh verification.

## 2. High — Material-interface averaging corrupts field results

**What goes wrong:** Solve two equal, 1 mm dielectric layers with relative permittivities 2.1 and 9.8 under 100 V. Their correct field magnitudes are approximately **82,353 V/m** and **17,647 V/m**.

On a conforming in-memory mesh, the exported field averaged **50,000 V/m** at the interface and approximately **68,010 V/m** at nodes 0.25 mm inside the first material, where the field should still be 82,353 V/m. Viewer fields and probes consume these smoothed values without a warning.

**Why:** `gauss_to_nodes()` averages every adjacent element into one nodal value, ignoring material boundaries. Electric-field components can legitimately jump across dielectric interfaces. The same routine processes `J` and Joule heat density, which can also be discontinuous.

**Suggested fix:** Recover fields separately within each material region. Preserve separate interface-side values for rendering, and calculate probe fields from the owning element’s potential gradient and material properties.

## 3. Medium — Convection-only temperature rise uses the wrong datum

**What goes wrong:** In the default busbar, change every thermal boundary to convection with `h = 10 W/m²K` and ambient temperature 20 °C. An in-memory reproduction returned:

- Maximum temperature rise above ambient: **116.924 K**
- Displayed `t_rise_k`: **0.167 K**

The job still reports convergence.

**Why:** When no fixed-temperature face exists, line 262 subtracts the solved minimum temperature from the maximum. This measures internal temperature variation, excluding the nearly uniform warming above ambient. It also becomes the mesh-study quantity.

**Suggested fix:** For a common convection ambient, calculate rise relative to that ambient. For multiple thermal reference temperatures, define the datum explicitly or report absolute maximum temperature. If retaining `Tmax − Tmin`, label it “temperature span.”

## 4. Medium — Surface-charge loading produces a false capacitance

**What goes wrong:** Keep the default capacitor’s 0 V and 100 V electrodes, then set `x0` to `surface-charge` with `0.001 C/m²`. The reported capacitance changes from approximately **23.242 pF** to **476.758 pF**, although the geometry and permittivity are unchanged.

**Why:** Capacitance is calculated whenever there are exactly two prescribed voltage levels. The high-voltage electrode’s reaction includes the charge induced by the additional surface-charge boundary. Dividing that total reaction by voltage includes this offset; it does not extract capacitance. Disabling the analytic reference does not suppress the misleading card.

**Suggested fix:** Suppress the scalar capacitance metric when additional nonzero charge sources exist, or calculate differential capacitance using an auxiliary excitation. Apply the equivalent restriction to resistance extraction when independent current sources exist.

## 5. Medium — Prescribed-current cases display the ground reaction as total current

**What goes wrong:** Set the busbar’s `x0` role to `current`, prescribe `1 A`, retain `x1 = 0 V`, and disable Joule heating. The solver correctly returns face currents of **+1 A at x0** and **−1 A at x1**, but the general current card displays **−1 A**.

**Why:** `high_flow` sums reactions on faces at the highest *prescribed* voltage. Here the sole prescribed-voltage face is ground, so `current_a` becomes its outgoing reaction rather than the supplied current.

**Suggested fix:** Define the general current metric using the driving terminal and an explicit direction, or display a nonnegative through-current magnitude. Preserve signed face currents separately.
